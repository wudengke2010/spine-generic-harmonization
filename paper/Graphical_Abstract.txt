Across 43 sites and three vendors, we benchmarked five harmonisation methods for spinal cord quantitative MRI. Harmonisation reduced vendor effects to statistical negligibility on variance-decomposition metrics, but a residual vendor signature remained machine-detectable for every pipeline. In the decontamination-preservation trade-off (healthy-control cohort), all five methods were mutually non-dominated; ComBat-joint offered a favourable descriptive balance in this dataset, whereas LME/FE failed to centre the held-out vendor under the specified transductive rule. Subject-ranking preservation and age/sex associations were evaluated for all methods; residual vendor-related confounding should be reported alongside any pooled analysis.
