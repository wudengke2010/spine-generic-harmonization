\section*{Abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Objectives:} Quantitative spinal cord MRI biomarkers vary systematically with scanner vendor, which limits multi-centre pooling, yet no harmonisation benchmark exists for spinal cord data. We benchmarked five post-hoc harmonisation pipelines on the open spine-generic dataset.

\noindent\textbf{Methods:} Eight biomarkers (T2w-CSA, GM-CSA, MTR, MTsat, FA, MD, AD, RD) were extracted from 267 participants scanned at 43 sites on three vendors (site and vendor perfectly nested). ComBat, ComBat-joint, CovBat, RELIEF and a mixed-effects/fixed-effects hybrid (LME/FE) were compared on four pre-specified endpoints---vendor-effect removal, biological-covariate recovery, subject-level preservation, and machine-learnability of the vendor signature---in healthy-control ($N=188$) and full-cohort ($N=246$) complete-case samples. Leave-one-vendor-out validation tested transfer to a held-out manufacturer under distribution matching.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} Vendor effects were large at baseline and dwarfed age- and sex-related variance. Vendor variance fell to near zero by construction, but a stricter, leakage-free test---machine detection of vendor from the biomarker profile---showed that a residual vendor signature persists for every pipeline, smallest for CovBat and largest for LME/FE. Age associations in diffusion metrics became detectable after harmonisation, and classification of radiological cord-compression labels was numerically higher but not statistically significant; the latter is an exploratory endpoint substantially confounded by age. Pipelines traded multivariate vendor removal against subject-level preservation, forming a frontier in the healthy-control cohort only. All four ComBat-family pipelines transferred to a held-out vendor without material degradation, whereas LME/FE left that vendor uncentred.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion:} For pooled multi-vendor spinal cord qMRI datasets of comparable structure, ComBat-family harmonisation, joint estimation in particular, may be appropriate as a preprocessing step, provided the residual vendor signature and the site-nested design are reported alongside pooled analyses.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{Key Points:}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item Scanner vendor effects are the dominant source of variance in multi-centre spinal cord qMRI, exceeding age and sex effects by one to two orders of magnitude, so harmonisation should be considered before pooling.
  \item All five benchmarked pipelines drive vendor variance to near zero by construction, but a stricter machine-learning test shows that residual vendor signatures remain, and, in the healthy-control cohort, pipelines that removed more vendor signal also gave lower subject-ranking preservation.
  \item Under leave-one-vendor-out testing with distribution matching, the four ComBat-family pipelines transferred to an unseen vendor without material degradation, whereas the mixed-effects/fixed-effects hybrid left the held-out vendor's offset intact.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Introduction}

Quantitative MRI (qMRI) biomarkers of the spinal cord---cross-sectional area (CSA), magnetisation transfer ratio (MTR), magnetisation transfer saturation (MTsat), and diffusion tensor metrics (FA, MD, AD, RD)---provide non-invasive windows into atrophy, demyelination, and axonal integrity [1,2], with applications in multiple sclerosis, degenerative cervical myelopathy, and spinal cord injury, and with dedicated normative databases enabling comparison against healthy morphometry [3]. Multi-centre consortia are increasingly adopting these biomarkers, but systematic vendor-dependent variability limits cross-site pooling [4,5]. A recent pan-European survey by the European Society of Radiology documented marked heterogeneity in MRI quality assurance and control practices across 37 countries [6], underscoring the urgency of robust harmonisation.

Even under standardised acquisition protocols, differences in gradient hardware, RF coil geometry, and vendor-specific reconstruction pipelines introduce offsets that often exceed the biological effect sizes of interest [4,7]. Statistical post-hoc harmonisation methods (ComBat [8--10], CovBat [11], RELIEF [12], and linear mixed-effects models [13]) have been extensively benchmarked in brain imaging, reviewed in the broader context of multicentre radiomics [14], and addressed in consensus recommendations for multicentre multiple sclerosis MRI [15], but remain uncharacterised for spinal cord qMRI. Fortin et al.\ first applied ComBat to multi-site DTI [10], establishing a framework later extended to cortical thickness [9], longitudinal harmonisation [16], covariance harmonisation [11], latent-factor removal [12], and out-of-sample harmonisation for new sites [17]. Middleton et al.\ demonstrated ComBat's effectiveness for spinal cord DTI [18], and Jodoin et al.\ provided deployment guidance for diffusion MRI [19], but no study has systematically compared the full ComBat family across a comprehensive spinal cord biomarker panel using unified multi-endpoint evaluation.

The spinal cord presents distinct harmonisation challenges: small per-site sample sizes in the complete-case analysis sample (HC: median 5 subjects per site, range 2--8; ALL: median 6, range 2--11) challenge empirical Bayes shrinkage; strong inter-metric correlations (diffusion metrics are intrinsically coupled; MTR and MTsat share acquisition-dependent variance) motivate multivariate evaluation; and it has not been quantified how much biological-covariate and subject-level variation each method retains while removing vendor effects.

Here we benchmark five harmonisation pipelines on eight spinal cord qMRI biomarkers using four complementary endpoints: vendor-effect removal, biological-covariate recovery, subject-level preservation, and machine-learnability of the vendor signature. We extend prior work through: (i) formal comparison of the full ComBat family---ComBat, ComBat-joint, and the covariance and latent-factor extensions CovBat and RELIEF; (ii) multivariate evaluation via PERMANOVA; (iii) an explicit trade-off (Pareto) analysis of decontamination against subject-level preservation; and (iv) leave-one-vendor-out (transductive) validation to test generalisability.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Dataset and Cohorts}

The spine-generic multi-subject dataset (release r20231212) [1] comprises 267 subjects scanned at 43 sites across three vendors (GE Healthcare: 37 subjects at 6 sites; Philips: 50 subjects at 8 sites; Siemens Healthineers: 180 subjects at 29 sites) using a standardised consensus protocol [4]; the release used here adds seven subjects and one site to the 260 participants at 42 sites described in the original data descriptor [1]. The dataset composition and the eight-biomarker panel are illustrated in Figure~1a,b. Site and vendor are perfectly nested (each site operates a single scanner from one vendor); reported vendor effects thus represent the composite ``vendor-in-site'' effect. Of the 267 subjects, 21 (7.9\%) were missing at least one biomarker---in almost all cases because a contrast was not acquired at the site rather than because of quality-control exclusions---leaving 246 complete cases; of the 203 healthy controls, 188 were complete cases (missingness mechanisms are itemised in Section~2.8 and Supplementary Material~S1). The complete-case analysis sample comprised 40 sites (GE 5, Philips 8, Siemens 27). Demographics were balanced across vendors in the complete-case analysis sample (Table~1; all $p\geq0.203$).

Two pre-specified cohorts were analysed: HC ($N=188$ complete-case healthy controls) isolates pure vendor-driven variance; ALL ($N=246$ complete-case, including 56 mild cervical compression and 2 DCM) provides a more heterogeneous, full-cohort analysis. The distribution of these labels was balanced across vendors ($\chi^2\ p=0.661$). The compression labels shipped in the dataset's \texttt{participants.tsv} are radiological assessments only: two experienced radiologists screened the MRI scans for spinal cord compression and annotated 64 of the 267 subjects, mostly mild compression, without any clinical diagnosis [3]. Mild cervical compression is common with ageing and is not necessarily pathological, and with only two DCM cases the labelled group is essentially radiological mild compression; moreover, the biomarkers were extracted at C2--C3 rather than at the level of compression. All analyses involving these labels (Section~3.3) are therefore exploratory. Detailed cohort flow and missingness patterns are provided in Supplementary Material~S1.

\subsection{Biomarker Extraction and Processing}

Eight biomarkers spanning four acquisition families were extracted using the Spinal Cord Toolbox [20] (Figure~1b): T2w-CSA from T2-weighted structural imaging, MTR and MTsat from magnetisation transfer, and FA, MD, AD and RD from diffusion tensor imaging, all sampled at the C2--C3 level; and GM-CSA from T2\textsuperscript{*}-weighted imaging, averaged over every segmented cervical slice: the T2w-derived C2--C3 window was not propagated into the T2\textsuperscript{*} space, and the T2\textsuperscript{*} stack covers a shorter craniocaudal range than the T2w acquisition, so GM-CSA is a whole-coverage average rather than a level-restricted measurement.

All segmentations, vertebral labels and metric maps were generated de novo for this study; the manual disc labels and segmentations shipped in the dataset's \texttt{derivatives} were not used, so that every subject was processed by one uniform, fully automatic pipeline. The pipeline follows the spine-generic reference pipeline (\url{https://spine-generic.readthedocs.io}) with four deliberate differences: (i) the seven level-restricted biomarkers were sampled in a common C2--C3 window so that these seven biomarkers cover the same nominal vertebral window, whereas the reference pipeline samples MTR/MTsat and DTI metrics over C2--C5 and GM-CSA over C3--C4; (ii) spinal cord and grey matter segmentation used the current contrast-agnostic nnUNet models (\texttt{sct\_deepseg spinalcord}; \texttt{sct\_deepseg graymatter}, model release r20250420) rather than the legacy \texttt{sct\_deepseg\_sc}/\texttt{sct\_deepseg\_gm}; (iii) vertebral labelling used \texttt{sct\_label\_vertebrae} on the T2w image in fully automatic mode, without the manual disc labels used by the reference pipeline (the consequences of this choice are audited in Supplementary Material~S2.2); and (iv) MT and diffusion metrics were averaged within the whole-cord mask rather than the PAM50 white-matter atlas. A step-by-step comparison with the reference pipeline is given in Supplementary Material~S2.

Because the installed SCT build (\texttt{7.4.dev0}) does not correspond to a numbered release, we report the exact source snapshot: master branch, git commit \texttt{93923f5e363d9400fbadab07459f125a08b21ad8} (2 June 2026, sixteen commits after the 7.3 release). The complete processing and analysis code is available at \url{https://github.com/wudengke2010/spine-generic-harmonization}.

Missing values arose from absent or incomplete acquisitions and, in three subjects, from the labelled C2--C3 level falling outside the MT field of view; diffusion metrics were unavailable for two of these subjects for the same reason, and for the third because the extracted FA values were identically zero and were set to missing by the aggregation-stage rule detailed in Supplementary Material~S2. No subject was excluded on the basis of a quality-control score (Supplementary Material~S1--S2).

\subsection{Harmonisation Methods}

Five harmonisation pipelines were evaluated (full mathematical details in Supplementary Material~S3). In all pipelines, scanner vendor (GE/Philips/Siemens, three levels) served as the batch variable; because sites are perfectly nested within vendors, the vendor effect is statistically confounded with site, and the composite vendor effect is the harmonisation target:

\textbf{ComBat} [8--10]: Per-metric location-and-scale adjustment with empirical Bayes shrinkage on vendor-specific parameters. Each biomarker harmonised independently.

\textbf{ComBat-joint} [9]: All eight biomarkers harmonised jointly, with a single shared empirical-Bayes prior across biomarkers, so that the eight metrics are adjusted as a set rather than independently. Cross-metric correlations enter the fit; whether they survive harmonisation is an empirical question, examined in Section~3.2.

\textbf{CovBat} [11]: ComBat-joint followed by principal component (PC) correction---residuals projected onto PCs, each PC harmonised independently, then projected back. PCs explaining at least 95\% of the residual variance were retained: $K=5$ of 8 (cumulative variance 95.9\% in the HC cohort, 95.6\% in the ALL cohort; the first three PCs accounted for 85.6\% and 84.9\%, respectively).

\textbf{RELIEF} [12]: ComBat-joint followed by independent component analysis (ICA) of residuals. Our implementation is a residual-ICA variant of RELIEF (REmoval of Latent Inter-scanner Effects through Factorization), rather than the linked matrix factorisation of the original report; this distinction is revisited in the Limitations. Components with significant vendor association (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-controlled ANOVA, $q<0.05$) are zeroed. In the present dataset, no component reached significance (all $q\geq0.99$), so no component was zeroed and the ICA step itself is inert. The pipeline is not, however, a lossless round-trip: reconstruction retains the five principal components that explain 95\% of the residual variance, so the three smallest components are discarded. Relative to ComBat-joint output in the HC cohort, RELIEF retained 94.5\% of total variance, with per-biomarker differences of up to 0.25 SD in GM-CSA and T2w-CSA. This truncation, not ICA, is the operative difference from ComBat-joint, and it is also what makes the held-out-vendor projection lossy (Section~3.6).

\textbf{LME/FE}: Vendor as a random intercept with age and sex fixed effects. When the random-effects covariance matrix was singular (expected with only 3 vendor levels, well below the recommended $k\geq5$ for stable REML estimation), the model fell back to ordinary least squares centring (9 of 16 biomarker$\times$cohort combinations, 56\%; see Results). Only MD and AD achieved stable LME fits in both cohorts. This hybrid is referred to below as LME/FE.

\subsection{Evaluation Endpoints}

Four complementary endpoints were evaluated per method per cohort (detailed in Supplementary Material~S4). Table~2 summarises what each endpoint measures, the desirable direction, and its main caveat.

\textbf{Vendor-effect removal}: Univariate $\eta^2$ (ANOVA) and multivariate PERMANOVA $R^2$ (Euclidean distance, 9,999 permutations) [21]. All five pipelines and the unharmonised baseline---including ComBat and LME/FE, which were refitted on the unified complete-case sample---were evaluated on the same subjects, with per-dimension z-scoring of biomarkers before PERMANOVA. Consequently, the multivariate $R^2$ equals the mean of the per-metric $\eta^2$ for every method (a mathematical identity of z-scored Euclidean PERMANOVA), so univariate and multivariate summaries are directly comparable throughout.

\textbf{Biological-covariate recovery}: Type-II semi-partial $R^2$ for age and sex from linear models. Because age and sex are modelled as protected covariates by every method, preservation of their modelled effects is partly expected by design; the informative quantities are the magnitude recovered, which differs between methods, and the per-metric significance patterns (Section~3.4).

\textbf{Subject-level preservation}: for each biomarker, Pearson $r$ across subjects between the pre- and post-harmonisation values---that is, preservation of the subject ranking on that biomarker---averaged over the eight biomarkers.

\textbf{Machine-learnability of the vendor signature}: Following the site-detection paradigm of Chen et al.\ [11], a random forest (500 trees, minimum leaf size 5, balanced class weights) was trained to classify scanner manufacturer (Siemens/GE/Philips) from the 8-biomarker profile of each subject, using repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation (20 repeats); reported AUC values are means $\pm$ SD across the repeats. To prevent information leakage, each harmonisation model was refitted on the training folds only and applied to the held-out fold within every cross-validation split. Harmonisation used vendor labels on training and test folds alike, as it would at deployment; the random forest, by contrast, received the eight biomarker values as its only input features and used the vendor labels solely as the outcome to be predicted. The macro one-vs-rest AUC quantifies residual vendor information detectable by machine learning, a stricter criterion than univariate $\eta^2$ because nonlinear combinations of features can encode vendor structure invisible to marginal statistics. As an exploratory sensitivity analysis, the same classifier, again using all eight biomarkers jointly as input features, was applied to the radiological compression labels (healthy control vs mild compression/DCM, ALL cohort, $N=246$ complete cases) and to sex labels. A shuffled-label permutation reference calibrated the chance level.

\subsection{Leave-One-Vendor-Out Transductive Validation}

For each method, harmonisation models were trained on subjects from two vendors and applied to the held-out third vendor in three rotation folds. Test-vendor transformation used z-score distribution matching (ComBat, ComBat-joint) or projection onto training PCA/ICA space (CovBat, RELIEF). LME/FE assigned offset=0 to unseen vendors. Three of the four endpoints (vendor-effect removal, biological-covariate recovery, and subject-level preservation; machine-learnability and the ALL-cohort compression-label and sex classification indices were not re-evaluated) were evaluated after pooling the harmonised training-vendor and held-out test-vendor data within each fold (Supplementary Material~S5). For ComBat, ComBat-joint, and LME/FE, the test-vendor transformation applies a per-biomarker affine (location--scale) map, so the test-vendor pre/post correlation equals 1.0 by construction; for CovBat and RELIEF, in contrast, projection onto the training PCA/ICA basis mixes biomarker dimensions, so the test-vendor pre/post correlation provides a genuine held-out-vendor distortion measure (Section~3.6).

LOVO preservation statistics for the first group therefore reflect the pooled sample rather than the unseen vendor in isolation, and the LOVO/internal $\eta^2$ ratio is likewise a pooled-sample quantity rather than a pure unseen-vendor measurement.

\subsection{Supplementary Analyses}

A reduced-imbalance subsample analysis restricted Siemens to 40 randomly drawn subjects per seed (HC cohort, retaining all GE and Philips subjects; $n\approx98$ per subset; residual vendor imbalance $\max/\min=1.60$ vs.\ $5.2$ in the full analysis; 20 random seeds), with all methods refitted per subset using the same canonical implementations as the main analysis, to isolate the effect of vendor imbalance from method performance. A true-equal-$n$ sensitivity analysis (25 subjects per vendor, $n=75$, imbalance ratio $=1.00$) is reported in Supplementary Material~S6. In addition, because only three vendor levels are available, ComBat was refitted with site (40 levels) or scanner model (12 levels) as the batch variable (Supplementary Material~S10; Section~3.8).

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}

Ninety-five per cent confidence intervals for key point estimates were obtained using a stratified nonparametric bootstrap ($B=2{,}000$), resampling subjects with replacement within each scanner-vendor stratum to preserve group proportions. Percentile intervals are reported for $r_{\text{pre,post}}$ and for the baseline vendor effect size. Because marginal CIs for $r_{\text{pre,post}}$ overlap across methods, method pairs were additionally compared with a within-resample paired bootstrap: for each bootstrap sample, all methods' $r$ values were recomputed and their differences tested against zero, which removes between-resample variance and directly targets the pairwise contrast. Bootstrap intervals are not reported for post-harmonisation $\eta^2$ or PERMANOVA $R^2$ because harmonisation methods optimise on the same data, rendering subject-level resampling intervals unreliable for these near-degenerate statistics.

\subsection{Reporting Guidelines and Missing Data}

This study follows STROBE reporting guidance for cross-sectional studies where applicable. Subjects with missing biomarker data were excluded via complete-case analysis (HC: $N=188/203$, 7.4\%; ALL: $N=246/267$, 7.9\%). Missingness was driven by site-level acquisition differences: two sites acquired no MT data at all (8 subjects), a further site lacked the second MT-off volume in 5 subjects, three subjects had their diffusion data split across multiple runs under a naming convention the pipeline did not detect, three lost the C2--C3 window after vertebral labelling, two had all-zero metric maps set to missing, and one had MT and diffusion processing not executed; one subject contributed to two of these categories, so they enumerate 21 unique subjects (Supplementary Material~S1). Incomplete subjects were older than complete subjects (median 34.0 vs.\ 28.5 years, $p=0.015$) though not different in sex ($p=0.880$). The mechanism was therefore not missing completely at random; the attribution of all 21 incomplete subjects is itemised in Supplementary Material~S1. A separate vertebral-labelling defect affecting 15 HC subjects is documented with a sensitivity analysis in Supplementary Material~S2.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Baseline Vendor Effects}

At baseline, vendor explained 31\% of univariate biomarker variance (mean $\eta^2=0.315$, 95\% CI [0.273, 0.381], stratified bootstrap; range 0.05--0.70 across metrics) and 31\% of multivariate structure (PERMANOVA $R^2=0.315$, $p=1\times10^{-4}$, the permutation lower bound with 9,999 permutations; HC cohort). These effects exceeded age-related variance by approximately two orders of magnitude and sex-related variance by one order. AD showed the largest vendor effect ($\eta^2_{\text{HC}}=0.70$), followed by MD ($0.46$) and MTsat ($0.34$); among structural metrics, GM-CSA showed the strongest vendor discrepancy ($\eta^2=0.18$, Philips scanners systematically lower; Supplementary Fig.~S3). In the ALL cohort, baseline vendor effects were comparable or larger ($\eta^2=0.336$; PERMANOVA $R^2=0.336$). In summary, vendor affiliation is the dominant source of biomarker variance at baseline, exceeding age-related variance by approximately two orders of magnitude.

\subsection{Vendor-Effect Removal}

All five pipelines reduced vendor $\eta^2$ to between $2.5\times10^{-4}$ (CovBat, HC) and $7.9\times10^{-4}$ (LME/FE, HC; mean $4.8\times10^{-4}$ across methods; Figure~2a,b, Table~3), corresponding to a 400- to 1,240-fold reduction from the baseline mean of 0.315 (mean $\approx2.8$ orders of magnitude across methods). For reference, the $\eta^2$ expected under the null hypothesis of no vendor effect is $(k-1)/(N-1)=2/187\approx0.011$; post-harmonisation values lie far below this chance level, so the effective reduction relative to chance expectation is 92.6--97.6\% across methods (LME/FE lowest at 92.6\%, CovBat highest at 97.6\%). Multivariate vendor structure became indistinguishable from chance (all PERMANOVA $p=1.0$ in both cohorts). These near-zero values should, however, be read as a process check rather than an independent validation: vendor is the harmonisation batch variable, and each method explicitly removes vendor location and scale, so a correctly implemented pipeline is expected to drive $\eta^2$ toward zero by construction. Machine-learnability (below) provides the stricter, non-degenerate criterion.

Covariance structure was displaced from the original to a comparable degree by all methods (Supplementary Fig.~S4). Expressed relative to the total Frobenius norm of the original cross-metric correlation matrix ($\|R_{\mathrm{orig}}\|_F=4.08$ in HC, $4.07$ in ALL), post-harmonisation correlation matrices lay 38.1--41.3\% away from the original in the HC cohort (ComBat 38.1\%, LME/FE 38.3\%, ComBat-joint 38.4\%, RELIEF 40.3\%, CovBat 41.3\%) and 40.2--43.0\% away in ALL (ComBat 40.2\%, ComBat-joint 40.5\%, LME/FE 40.6\%, RELIEF 42.3\%, CovBat 43.0\%). No method therefore preserved the covariance structure in absolute terms. Between-method distances were smaller but far from uniform across the ten method pairs: they ranged from 0.048 (ComBat vs.\ ComBat-joint) to 0.501 (CovBat vs.\ LME/FE) in HC and from 0.036 to 0.479 in ALL, against a mean method-to-original displacement of 1.60 (HC) and 1.68 (ALL). ComBat and ComBat-joint yielded near-identical correlation matrices (0.048 in HC, 0.036 in ALL), as did CovBat and RELIEF (0.094 and 0.078); the remaining eight pairs lay between 0.30 and 0.50. The location--scale methods remained marginally closer to the original than the covariance-projection methods. In summary, every method drives vendor variance far below chance expectation---a process check rather than independent validation---while displacing the original cross-metric covariance structure by 38--43\%.

\subsection{Machine-Learnability of the Vendor Signature}

Unharmonised biomarker profiles allowed near-perfect machine detection of scanner manufacturer (macro one-vs-rest AUC $=0.986\pm0.002$ in the ALL cohort; $0.983\pm0.002$ in HC), confirming that the vendor signature dominates the multivariate feature space. After harmonisation refitted within each cross-validation fold to prevent leakage of harmonisation parameters (Methods), detectability decreased substantially but remained above chance for every pipeline (permutation reference $0.495\pm0.038$): CovBat $0.629\pm0.020$, RELIEF $0.690\pm0.017$, ComBat-joint $0.704\pm0.018$, ComBat $0.706\pm0.016$, and LME/FE $0.811\pm0.015$ (ALL cohort, $N=246$; HC values were concordant: 0.670, 0.733, 0.756, 0.751, and 0.851, respectively). Expressed against the chance-adjusted detectability margin, location--scale harmonisation removed 36\% (LME/FE) to 73\% (CovBat) of the machine-detectable vendor information; the pipeline ordering parallels correction depth, the FE fallback being a pure location shift without scale adjustment. Per-vendor detectability after ComBat ranged from 0.67 (Philips) to 0.80 (GE) (Supplementary Table~S8).

Classification of the exploratory radiological compression labels (all eight biomarkers combined as input features; Methods) was numerically higher after harmonisation, from AUC $0.520\pm0.024$ (unharmonised) to $0.582$--$0.625$ across pipelines, highest for ComBat ($0.625\pm0.018$; values throughout are means $\pm$ SD over the 20 cross-validation repeats, each repeat contributing one AUC computed from its pooled out-of-fold predictions; the corresponding pooled point estimate for ComBat was $0.64$, 95\% CI $0.55$--$0.72$). Within-repeat paired comparison against Original gave mean $\Delta$AUC from $+0.062$ (CovBat) to $+0.105$ (ComBat). These paired tests treat the 20 repeats as the unit of analysis; because every repeat resamples the same 246 subjects, the resulting $p$-values are anti-conservative and the effect sizes, not the $p$-values, should carry the interpretation. The conservative unpaired Hanley--McNeil test on the single point estimate did not reach significance ($p=0.07$).

Two caveats bound this endpoint. First, the labels are radiological assessments of mostly mild cord compression rather than clinical diagnoses (Section~2.1). Second, the labelled group is substantially older than controls (median 34.0 vs.\ 28.0 years, $p=2\times10^{-7}$), and age alone discriminates the labels (AUC $=0.73$), above any biomarker-based classifier. We therefore repeated the classification with every biomarker residualised on age and sex, with the residualisation refitted inside each training fold: the numerical increase persisted (Original $0.563\pm0.022$, ComBat $0.654\pm0.022$; Supplementary Material~S9), so the increase is not fully explained by the modelled linear age and sex effects, although residual confounding by non-linear age effects, site or vendor composition, and unmeasured age-related change cannot be excluded. Sex classification was essentially unaffected (Original $0.646\pm0.020$; harmonised $0.662$--$0.670$), indicating that harmonisation removed vendor confounding without materially reducing sex-label classification performance. In summary, harmonisation removes most, but not all, of the machine-detectable vendor signature, and the pipeline ordering by residual detectability parallels correction depth.

\subsection{Biological-Covariate Recovery}

Before harmonisation, no biomarker showed a significant age association in either cohort (HC: all $p\geq0.34$; ALL: all $p\geq0.19$). After harmonisation, significant age associations emerged selectively in the diffusion metrics: MD and AD in both cohorts (all methods; HC $p=0.006$--$0.049$, ALL $p\leq0.0019$) and additionally RD in ALL ($p\leq0.007$); structural and magnetisation-transfer metrics remained non-significant (all $p\geq0.18$). In mean terms, age $R^2$ increased 5.6--6.2-fold in HC (from $1.5\times10^{-3}$ to $8.7\times10^{-3}$--$9.5\times10^{-3}$) and 4.8--5.0-fold in ALL (from $3.2\times10^{-3}$ to $1.5\times10^{-2}$--$1.6\times10^{-2}$); these fold changes should be read alongside the absolute values and the per-metric significance above, since a ratio of two near-zero variances is itself unstable. Sex $R^2$ showed a similar pattern (1.1--1.3-fold increase). Absolute age effects were larger in ALL, consistent with its wider age range and phenotypic heterogeneity, but remained below 2\% of variance. In summary, age associations become detectable in the diffusion metrics after harmonisation, a pattern consistent with the removal of vendor confounding; the design cannot establish that the recovered variance is biological rather than residual structure.

\subsection{Subject-Level Preservation and the Pareto Frontier}

Methods diverged on subject-ranking preservation (HC cohort, mean $r$ across methods: LME/FE $=0.816$, ComBat-joint $=0.806$, RELIEF $=0.791$, ComBat $=0.789$, CovBat $=0.782$). This ranking was highly concordant between cohorts (Spearman $\rho=0.90$), with a single adjacent exchange of ranks (ComBat and RELIEF swapped ranks 3--4; ALL values: LME/FE 0.801, ComBat-joint 0.787, ComBat 0.774, RELIEF 0.772, CovBat 0.763). Although the marginal bootstrap 95\% CIs for $r_{\text{pre,post}}$ overlap across methods (Table~3), a within-resample paired bootstrap ($B=2{,}000$) confirmed that the preservation hierarchy is statistically reliable: 8 of 10 method pairs were significantly separated in HC (all $p\leq0.003$) and 9 of 10 in ALL, with LME/FE significantly highest and CovBat significantly lowest (the only non-separated pairs were ComBat vs.\ CovBat and ComBat vs.\ RELIEF in HC, and ComBat vs.\ RELIEF in ALL; Supplementary Table~S7).

In the HC cohort a Pareto frontier emerged: all five methods were mutually non-dominated, so a pipeline could buy greater multivariate decontamination only by giving up subject-level preservation (Figure~3a). In the full cohort no such frontier exists---the decontamination axis there is compressed to $2.3$--$3.0\times10^{-4}$ and every ComBat-family method is dominated by LME/FE on both axes (Figure~3b)---so that panel is read as a trade-off plot rather than a frontier. Because post-harmonisation PERMANOVA $R^2$ values are near-degenerate in general (Section~3.2), we repeated the ordering using leakage-free vendor detectability (Section~3.3) as the decontamination axis: the corner solutions were preserved (CovBat: maximal removal; LME/FE: maximal preservation), and ComBat and ComBat-joint were indistinguishable on removal in HC (0.751 vs.\ 0.756, within cross-validation noise) with ComBat-joint showing higher preservation (0.806 vs.\ 0.789); in the full cohort, ComBat-joint weakly dominated ComBat on both axes (lower detectability, 0.704 vs.\ 0.706, and higher preservation, 0.787 vs.\ 0.774), although the removal difference again lies within cross-validation noise. LME/FE's top rank partly reflects its minimal correction: the FE fallback applies a pure location shift without scale adjustment, leaving subject-level variance essentially untouched.

Under the reduced-imbalance subsample ($n\approx98$ per seed, imbalance ratio $1.60$ vs.\ $5.2$ in the full analysis), the pooled-$r$ preservation hierarchy was retained (LME/FE $r=0.779\pm0.011$, ComBat-joint $0.773\pm0.014$, RELIEF $0.760\pm0.015$, CovBat $0.749\pm0.016$, ComBat $0.745\pm0.015$). The only rank change was the adjacent exchange of ComBat and CovBat, a pair not significantly separated in the main analysis. A true-equal-$n$ sensitivity analysis (25 subjects per vendor, $n=75$, ratio $1.00$) gave concordant results (LME/FE $0.772\pm0.015$; ComBat-family $0.732$--$0.768$).

Absolute values were lower than in the full cohort ($0.782$--$0.816$) because both subsample designs carried a higher baseline vendor-variance fraction: the measured baseline $\eta^2$ was $0.374$ (range $0.343$--$0.401$ across seeds) under reduced imbalance and $0.389$ (range $0.337$--$0.431$) under exact balance, versus $0.315$ in the full analysis. Because pooled $r$ after a location--scale correction cannot exceed $\sqrt{1-\eta^2}$, these baselines imply ceilings of approximately $0.79$ and $0.78$, respectively; every per-method mean fell below its design's ceiling, and the per-seed LME/FE estimate never exceeded the per-seed ceiling in any of the 20 seeds of either design (Supplementary Material~S6). Within-vendor subject rankings were nonetheless preserved near-perfectly by every method in both subsample designs (mean within-vendor $r\geq0.97$), indicating that the pooled-$r$ gradient reflects differential removal of between-vendor variance rather than degradation of within-vendor subject rankings (Supplementary Material~S6). The preservation hierarchy therefore persists under reduced imbalance and under exact balance, indicating that it tracks correction depth rather than the dataset's vendor imbalance; this ordering nonetheless remains a descriptive characterisation of this dataset's structure rather than a universal ranking.

\subsection{LOVO Transductive Validation}

Four of the five pipelines transferred to an unseen vendor without material degradation; only LME/FE failed. Pooled within-fold vendor $\eta^2$ after LOVO was $1.8\times10^{-4}$ for ComBat, $4.4\times10^{-4}$ for ComBat-joint, $8.9\times10^{-4}$ for RELIEF and $1.0\times10^{-3}$ for CovBat, all within a factor of four of their internal values (LOVO/internal ratios 0.5, 0.9, 2.0 and 4.0), with PERMANOVA $R^2$ numerically identical and $p>0.99$ in every fold (Figure~4a,b). LME/FE, by contrast, left $\eta^2$ at $0.199$, 250-fold its internal value, with PERMANOVA $p=0.0001$ in all three folds: the FE fallback assigns an offset of zero to a vendor it has never seen, so the held-out vendor keeps its full offset by construction.

The four ComBat-family pipelines differ in mechanism rather than in outcome. All remove the held-out vendor's location and scale at test time, either per biomarker (ComBat, ComBat-joint), per principal component (CovBat) or per independent component after projection onto the training basis (RELIEF). CovBat and RELIEF, the two pipelines that project a new vendor into a basis estimated on the training vendors, did not preserve held-out-vendor subject rankings exactly (fold-wise $r=0.84$--$0.95$; fold means 0.90 for both), whereas the per-biomarker affine pipelines did so by construction. The distortion is small in absolute terms but systematic, and it is the price of projecting an unseen vendor into a learned subspace.

Two qualifications apply. First, this is a transductive evaluation: the test-vendor transformation matches the held-out vendor to the training distribution using that vendor's own mean and variance, so it estimates transfer under distribution matching, not performance for a single incoming subject whose vendor is unknown (Section~4). Second, per-biomarker ComBat was fitted without empirical-Bayes shrinkage in the LOVO analysis (\texttt{eb=False}), because with two training batches the EB variance estimate for a single biomarker is not identifiable; the internal analysis used \texttt{eb=True}. Its LOVO/internal ratio of 0.5 therefore reflects that difference in estimator and should not be read as an improvement on unseen vendors.

\subsection{Site- and Scanner-Model-Level Harmonisation}

Because only three vendor levels are available, we additionally refitted per-metric ComBat (empirical Bayes shrinkage) in the HC cohort using site (40 levels) or scanner model (12 levels) as the batch variable (Supplementary Material~S10). Site-level harmonisation reduced the residual vendor effect to the chance level ($\eta^2=0.012$, vs.\ the chance expectation of $\approx0.011$; site-level $\eta^2$ fell from $0.54$ to $0.045$) and recovered slightly more age-related variance (semi-partial $R^2=0.011$ vs.\ $0.009$ for vendor-batch ComBat), at the cost of lower subject-level preservation ($r=0.703$ vs.\ $0.789$). Scanner-model harmonisation was intermediate (vendor $\eta^2=0.0022$; $r=0.763$). Thus finer-grained batch definitions remain viable when the vendor count is small, but they neither resolve the vendor--site nesting nor transfer to unseen vendors or sites, and in this dataset the finer-grained definitions gave lower subject-ranking preservation; given the sparse per-site sample sizes and the nested design, these comparisons should be read as exploratory.

\section{Discussion}

This study provides, to our knowledge, the first systematic benchmark of statistical harmonisation methods for multi-vendor spinal cord qMRI biomarkers. Six principal findings emerge.

First, baseline vendor effects in spinal cord qMRI are large ($\eta^2=0.31$, PERMANOVA $R^2=0.315$), comparable to or exceeding brain imaging reports [9--11], exceeding age-related variance by approximately two orders of magnitude and sex-related variance by one order. This suggests that vendor offsets, if uncontrolled, would confound biomarker contrasts across vendors in any pooled analysis. Physical harmonisation, such as vendor-specific recalibration of magnetisation transfer metrics [7], offers a complementary acquisition-level route but requires prospective protocol control; statistical post-hoc harmonisation, as benchmarked here, applies retrospectively to existing consortia data. These results motivate harmonisation as a candidate preprocessing step for multi-centre spinal cord studies of similar structure (three vendors, perfectly nested sites).

Second, all five pipelines reduce vendor effects to statistical negligibility on the variance-decomposition metrics, which they drive towards zero by construction ($\eta^2$, PERMANOVA), whereas leakage-free machine-learnability, a criterion no method optimises directly, reveals substantial but incomplete removal (AUC 0.63--0.81 across methods, full cohort; HC 0.67--0.85; versus 0.99 unharmonised and 0.50 chance). We note that evaluating vendor detectability after full-sample harmonisation, as is common practice, is optimistically biased because the harmonisation parameters absorb test-fold structure; our fold-internal protocol (Methods) quantifies the deployment-relevant residual.

The choice among methods therefore hinges not on whether they work, but on the downstream analytic goal. For subject-level inference within a fixed set of scanners (longitudinal change detection), ComBat-joint may be appropriate; LME/FE preserves subject rankings best but leaves the largest residual vendor signature (AUC 0.81) and cannot centre a vendor absent from training, so it is not suitable for individual prediction on data from a new scanner. For pooled-group analyses where maximal multivariate vendor removal is the priority, CovBat achieved the lowest residual vendor AUC (0.629 ALL), albeit at the cost of the lowest subject-level preservation ($r=0.782$ HC); RELIEF was intermediate. Our machine-learnability results quantify this hierarchy directly in spinal cord data: despite reducing vendor $\eta^2$ to between $2.5\times10^{-4}$ and $7.9\times10^{-4}$, LME/FE left the most detectable residual signature (AUC 0.81), whereas CovBat retained the least (0.63). The residual detectability of ComBat-harmonised profiles (AUC 0.71) is in the range previously reported for location-scale harmonisation in multi-site brain imaging [11], although direct numerical comparison across modalities is constrained by differences in feature space, sample size, and number of sites.

Third, the trade-off between decontamination and preservation is reproducible across cohorts and persisted under the reduced-imbalance and true-equal-$n$ subsample analyses (Section~3.5). It is a genuine Pareto frontier only in the HC cohort: in the full cohort the decontamination axis is degenerate (PERMANOVA $R^2$ between $2.3$ and $3.0\times10^{-4}$ for all four ComBat-family pipelines), so Figure~3b is a trade-off plot rather than a frontier, and that cohort's ordering becomes interpretable only when leakage-free detectability is used as the decontamination axis. The higher baseline vendor-variance fractions of these subsamples ($\eta^2=0.374$ and $0.389$ vs.\ $0.315$) imply correspondingly lower pooled-$r$ ceilings ($\sqrt{1-\eta^2}\approx0.79$ and $0.78$), which accounts for their lower absolute $r$ values. The frontier should nonetheless be read as a descriptive characterisation of this dataset's structure rather than a universal method ranking.

Fourth, the LOVO transductive evaluation identified a single failure under the specified deployment rule. With the test-vendor transformation applied on the same terms to all five pipelines, the four ComBat-family pipelines transferred to an unseen vendor within a factor of four of their internal vendor effect ($\eta^2$ from $1.8\times10^{-4}$ to $1.0\times10^{-3}$), whereas LME/FE left the held-out vendor uncentred ($\eta^2=0.199$). The contrast therefore separates a property of the method from a property of the deployment rule: what distinguishes these pipelines is whether they can apply a transformation to a vendor absent from training, not whether they model latent structure. Because the held-out vendor is matched to the training distribution using its own mean and variance, this is a transductive evaluation and bounds what distribution matching can achieve, not what a prospective deployment to an unknown scanner would. For consortia that expect to add vendor scanners, our results are consistent with ComBat-family harmonisation, joint estimation in particular, being the most practical route, subject to that caveat and to the single-dataset nature of the observation. Out-of-sample harmonisation for new sites is an active methodological frontier: ComBat-Predict provides a principled framework for applying trained harmonisation models to unseen sites without re-accessing reference data [17], and our LOVO results provide preliminary within-dataset support for ComBat-family harmonisation, joint estimation in particular, for this deployment scenario.

In practical deployment, a consortium would fit the harmonisation model on the pooled reference data, store the fitted vendor parameters, and apply the transformation to incoming subjects, whose vendor labels must be known: an affine location--scale map for the ComBat family (as supported by our LOVO folds), or projection onto the reference PCA/ICA basis for CovBat and RELIEF. When a scanner from a new vendor joins, ComBat-Predict [17] avoids re-accessing the reference data altogether, although our results caution that this route is supported only for the ComBat family. Where several sites per vendor exist, harmonising with site or scanner model as the batch variable is a viable finer-grained alternative (Section~3.8): it removed vendor effects to the chance level in our data but gave lower subject-ranking preservation, a difference that should be read as exploratory given the sparse per-site samples and the nested design, and it cannot transfer to unseen sites. Whichever route is chosen, our leakage-free detectability results imply that pooled analyses should re-estimate and report the residual vendor signature (vendor AUC) as a routine deployment check rather than assume vendor effects have been eliminated.

Fifth, the structural constraints revealed by this benchmark---vendor-site nesting, LME singular covariance in 56\% of cases, and RELIEF ICA inactivation---reflect the 3-vendor, perfectly-nested spine-generic design. These are not methodological failures but intrinsic dataset properties that bound interpretability: reported vendor effects cannot be partitioned into vendor-level versus site-level components, and LME/RELIEF's degradation in low-vendor-count settings is expected given their modelling assumptions. Larger consortia with more vendors and crossed designs would be expected to relax these constraints, by providing enough vendor levels for stable REML and richer residual structure for ICA.

Sixth, biological-covariate recovery after harmonisation was selective and interpretable: age associations absent at baseline (all $p\geq0.19$) became significant in the diffusion metrics (Section~3.4; mean fold increase 5.6--6.2 in HC and 4.8--5.0 in ALL), a pattern consistent with harmonisation removing vendor confounding that had obscured these associations; the design cannot, however, establish that the recovered variance is biological signal rather than residual structure. The exploratory classification of radiological compression labels was also numerically higher (AUC 0.52 to 0.63), and the numerical increase persisted when every biomarker was residualised on age and sex within each training fold (Section~3.3, Supplementary Material~S9). This endpoint nonetheless cannot support disease-related conclusions: the labels are radiological assessments of mostly mild, age-related compression rather than clinical diagnoses, the biomarkers were sampled at C2--C3 rather than at the compressed level, and the analysis is internal to a single dataset. Prior prospective work does show that multiparametric qMRI---CSA, FA, MTR and T2\textsuperscript{*}-based metrics---detects subclinical tissue injury in asymptomatic cervical cord compression [22] and tract-specific degeneration rostral to the stenotic level in cervical spondylotic myelopathy [23], which makes the radiological label a biologically plausible, if weak, target; validation on an independent cohort with well-characterised clinical disease remains necessary.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) \emph{Dataset structure.} Vendor and site are perfectly nested, so vendor-level and site-level effects cannot be separated. Only three vendors were represented, with substantial imbalance (Siemens: 130/188 in HC, 166/246 in ALL); although the preservation hierarchy persisted under reduced-imbalance and true-equal-$n$ subsampling (Section~3.5), extrapolation to other manufacturers and more balanced consortia requires caution. Missingness was not modelled, so the older age of excluded subjects (median 34.0 vs.\ 28.5 years, $p=0.015$) is uncorrected.

(ii) \emph{Partial method activation.} LME converged in only 7 of 16 biomarker$\times$cohort combinations (44\%), so the reported LME/FE preservation advantage ($r=0.82$) may reflect the minimal correction of the FE fallback rather than LME shrinkage. RELIEF's ICA inactivation ($q\geq0.99$ for all components) may likewise be dataset-specific: Zhang et al.\ [12] demonstrated RELIEF's effectiveness in a three-site DTI study where latent vendor structure persisted after ComBat preprocessing, whereas our 43-site design with ComBat-joint preprocessing may already have exhausted that structure. In addition, our implementation is a residual-ICA variant rather than the linked matrix factorisation of the original report [12], so the present results do not exclude that the original formulation would have found transferable latent structure.

(iii) \emph{Scope of the held-out-vendor validation.} LOVO validation used z-score matching for unseen-vendor transformation, the standard ComBat-from-template approach [9], and does not test prospective acquisition on genuinely new hardware; principled out-of-sample frameworks such as ComBat-Predict [17] offer a natural extension. This analysis was also not pre-registered, although all harmonisation methods were evaluated on a pre-specified endpoint framework and the complete pipeline is publicly documented in the accompanying code repository.

(iv) \emph{Residual detectability.} The leakage-free evaluation assumes vendor labels are observed at deployment, the standard assumption of ComBat-style harmonisation. Residual vendor detectability remained above chance for every method (AUC up to 0.85 for LME/FE), so harmonised pooling is not vendor-blind and pooled analyses should continue to report vendor composition.

(v) \emph{Exploratory compression-label endpoint.} The compression labels are radiological assessments of mostly mild cervical compression made from MRI appearance alone [3], without clinical diagnosis; mild compression is common with ageing, the labelled group was substantially older than controls, and only two DCM cases were included. Biomarkers were sampled at C2--C3 rather than at the compressed level, and the classification was evaluated by internal cross-validation on a single dataset. Although the numerical increase persisted after age--sex residualisation (Supplementary Material~S9), the endpoint supports no disease-related conclusion, and external validation on clinically characterised cohorts is required.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Statistical harmonisation reduces vendor effects to statistical negligibility on variance-decomposition metrics and removes most, though not all, of the machine-detectable vendor signature in multi-centre spinal cord qMRI. It may be appropriate as a preprocessing step for datasets of similar structure (three vendors, perfectly nested sites), with residual vendor-related confounding reported alongside pooled analyses. ComBat-joint offered the most favourable descriptive trade-off in our sample: within-sample vendor removal was comparable to per-metric ComBat ($\eta^2=4.9$ vs.\ $4.1\times10^{-4}$, both far below the chance level) and the two methods were indistinguishable on leakage-free vendor detectability (AUC 0.704 vs.\ 0.706 in ALL), yet ComBat-joint preserved subject-level information slightly better ($r=0.806$ vs.\ 0.789 in HC). ComBat-joint may therefore be considered for multicentre datasets of similar structure; per-metric ComBat remains appropriate when individual biomarkers are harmonised in isolation.
