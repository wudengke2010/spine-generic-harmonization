ABSTRACT — Scanner Vendor Harmonisation of Spinal Cord Quantitative MRI Biomarkers: A Five-Method Benchmark

Yilin Zhu¹, Dengke Wu²
¹ Changsha Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Changsha No. 8 Hospital), Changsha, Hunan, China
² Department of Emergency Medicine, and Emergency Medicine and Difficult Diseases Institute, The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, Changsha 410011, Hunan, China

OBJECTIVES: Quantitative spinal cord MRI biomarkers vary systematically with scanner vendor, which limits multi-centre pooling, yet no harmonisation benchmark exists for spinal cord data. We benchmarked five post-hoc harmonisation methods on the open spine-generic dataset.

METHODS: Eight biomarkers (T2w-CSA, GM-CSA, MTR, MTsat, FA, MD, AD, RD) were extracted from 267 participants scanned at 43 sites on three vendors (site and vendor perfectly nested). ComBat, ComBat-joint, CovBat, RELIEF and a mixed-effects/fixed-effects hybrid (LME/FE) were compared on four pre-specified endpoints — vendor-effect removal, biological-covariate recovery, subject-level preservation, and machine-learnability of the vendor signature — in healthy-control (N = 188) and pathology-inclusive (N = 246) complete-case cohorts. Leave-one-vendor-out validation tested generalisation to unseen manufacturers.

RESULTS: Vendor effects were large at baseline and dwarfed age- and sex-related variance. All methods reduced vendor variance to near zero on the metrics they optimise, but a stricter, leakage-free test — machine detection of vendor from the biomarker profile — showed that a residual vendor signature persists for every method, smallest for CovBat and largest for LME/FE. Harmonisation unmasked age associations in diffusion metrics that were undetectable at baseline, and improved classification of radiological cord-compression labels, an exploratory endpoint that is substantially confounded by age. Methods traded multivariate vendor removal against subject-level preservation along a Pareto frontier. Only ComBat and ComBat-joint generalised to an unseen vendor without significant degradation, and joint estimation degraded least.

CONCLUSION: For pooled multi-vendor spinal cord qMRI datasets of comparable structure, ComBat-family harmonisation, joint estimation in particular, may be appropriate as a preprocessing step, provided the residual vendor signature and the site-nested design are reported alongside pooled analyses.

KEYWORDS: Spinal cord; Quantitative MRI; Harmonisation; ComBat; Multicentre study; Scanner vendor effects
