ABSTRACT — Scanner Vendor Harmonisation of Spinal Cord Quantitative MRI Biomarkers: A Five-Method Benchmark

Yilin Zhu¹, Dengke Wu²
¹ Changsha Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Changsha No. 8 Hospital), Changsha, Hunan, China
² Department of Emergency Medicine, and Emergency Medicine and Difficult Diseases Institute, The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, Changsha 410011, Hunan, China

OBJECTIVES: Quantitative spinal cord MRI biomarkers vary systematically with scanner vendor, which limits multi-centre pooling, yet no harmonisation benchmark exists for spinal cord data.

METHODS: Eight biomarkers (T2w-CSA, GM-CSA, MTR, MTsat, FA, MD, AD, RD) were extracted from the open spine-generic dataset (267 participants, 43 sites, three vendors; site and vendor perfectly nested): seven at the C2–C3 level and GM-CSA averaged over the segmented cervical cord. Five post-hoc methods — ComBat, ComBat-joint, CovBat, RELIEF and a mixed-effects/fixed-effects hybrid (LME/FE) — were benchmarked on four endpoints in healthy-control (N = 188) and pathology-inclusive (N = 246) cohorts: vendor-effect removal, biological-signal recovery, subject-level preservation, and machine-learnability of the vendor signature. Leave-one-vendor-out validation tested generalisation to unseen manufacturers.

RESULTS: Baseline vendor effects were large (η² = 0.31; PERMANOVA R² = 0.315, p = 1×10⁻⁴). All methods reduced the vendor variance fraction to 2.5–7.9×10⁻⁴, but this criterion was nearly degenerate because vendor is by construction the harmonisation variable. In leakage-free evaluation, machine detection of vendor fell from an AUC of about 0.99 to 0.63–0.81, while pathology classification improved from 0.52 to 0.62. Biological-signal recovery was selective: age associations absent at baseline became significant in diffusion metrics after harmonisation (p ≤ 0.0019). Methods diverged on subject-level preservation (r = 0.78–0.82), forming a Pareto frontier. Only ComBat and ComBat-joint generalised to an unseen vendor without significant degradation (PERMANOVA p ≥ 0.97); RELIEF and LME/FE did not (η² ≈ 0.2, p < 0.01); ComBat-joint degraded least (LOVO/internal η² ratio 0.9 vs. 6.0). Both failures trace to three vendors with nested sites.

CONCLUSION: Harmonisation may be appropriate for pooled multi-vendor spinal cord quantitative MRI datasets of comparable structure. ComBat-joint, which harmonises all eight biomarkers jointly, provided the most stable generalisation to unseen vendors and the best subject-level preservation among ComBat-family methods, and may warrant preference for broad deployment.

KEYWORDS: Spinal cord; Quantitative MRI; Harmonisation; ComBat; Multicentre study; Scanner vendor effects
