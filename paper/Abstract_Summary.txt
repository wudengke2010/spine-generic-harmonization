ABSTRACT — Scanner Vendor Harmonisation of Spinal Cord Quantitative MRI Biomarkers: A Five-Method Benchmark

Yilin Zhu, Dengke Wu
Department of Emergency Medicine, and Emergency Medicine and Difficult Diseases Institute, The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, Changsha 410011, Hunan, China

OBJECTIVES: Quantitative spinal cord MRI biomarkers exhibit systematic vendor-dependent variability that limits multi-centre pooling, yet no systematic harmonisation benchmark exists for spinal cord data.

METHODS: Eight qMRI biomarkers (T2w-CSA, GM-CSA, MTR, MTsat, FA, MD, AD, RD) were extracted at C2–C3 from the spine-generic dataset (267 subjects, 43 sites, 3 vendors; site and vendor perfectly nested). Five methods — ComBat, ComBat-joint, CovBat, RELIEF, LME/FE — were evaluated on four endpoints in healthy-control (N = 188) and pathology-inclusive (N = 246) cohorts: vendor-effect removal, biological-signal recovery, subject-level preservation, and machine-learnability of the vendor signature. Leave-one-vendor-out validation tested generalisability.

RESULTS: Baseline vendor effects were substantial (η² = 0.31; PERMANOVA R² = 0.315, p = 1×10⁻⁴, the permutation lower bound with 9,999 permutations). All methods reduced vendor η² to between 2.5×10⁻⁴ and 7.9×10⁻⁴ (HC cohort; 92.6–97.6% reduction relative to the chance level (k−1)/(N−1) ≈ 0.011, equivalently ≥99.7% relative to the observed baseline η² = 0.315; ALL: 96.3–97.2% vs chance, ≥99.9% vs observed) — a near-degenerate criterion, since vendor is the harmonisation batch variable. In leakage-free fold-internal evaluation, random-forest vendor detection fell from AUC ≈ 0.99 (unharmonised) to 0.63–0.81 across methods in the pathology-inclusive cohort (HC: 0.67–0.85; permutation reference 0.50), demonstrating substantial but incomplete removal of the multivariate vendor signature, lowest for CovBat and highest for LME/FE; pathology classification concurrently improved (AUC 0.52 to 0.62, ComBat; paired p < 0.001). Age associations absent at baseline (all p ≥ 0.19) became significant in diffusion metrics after harmonisation (MD/AD, p ≤ 0.0019 in the pathology-inclusive cohort). Methods diverged on subject preservation (LME/FE: r = 0.82; CovBat: r = 0.78), forming a Pareto frontier; within-resample paired bootstrap confirmed the preservation hierarchy (8/10 and 9/10 method pairs significantly separated in HC and ALL, respectively). LOVO validation showed ComBat/ComBat-joint generalised without significant degradation (PERMANOVA p ≥ 0.97 in all folds), while RELIEF and LME/FE failed (η² ≈ 0.2, p < 0.01). Three methodological constraints — vendor-site nesting, LME fixed-effects fallback (56% of internal cases), and RELIEF ICA inactivation — converge on a single structural limitation: only 3 vendors in a perfectly nested 43-site design.

CONCLUSION: Harmonisation may be appropriate for pooled multi-vendor spinal cord qMRI datasets of similar structure (three vendors, perfectly nested sites). ComBat-joint, which harmonises all eight biomarkers jointly, provided the most stable generalisation to unseen scanner vendors (LOVO/internal η² ratio 0.9 vs. 6.0 for per-metric ComBat) together with the highest subject-level preservation among ComBat-family methods, and may be appropriate for broad deployment. Per-metric ComBat remains appropriate when individual biomarkers are harmonised in isolation, and CovBat/RELIEF suit pooled-group analyses within fixed vendor sets.

KEYWORDS: Spinal cord; Quantitative MRI; Harmonisation; ComBat; Multicentre study; Scanner vendor effects
